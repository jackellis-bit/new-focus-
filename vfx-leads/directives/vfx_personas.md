# VFX Buyer Personas

> Knowledge base for VFX house sales targeting. Defines who our buyers are at VFX facilities, what they care about, and how to approach each tier.

## The Buyer Triangle

Every serious opportunity at a VFX house should have:
1. **One Economic Buyer**
2. **One Technical Champion**
3. **One active User group**

If any of those are missing, it's not a qualified deal.

**Shortcut framing:**
- Sell value to Post/Production leadership
- Prove capability with Supervisors
- Drive adoption through Artists

That triangle is how VFX houses actually buy.

---

## Tier 1: Economic Buyers (Sign Budget / Approve Vendors)

These are your primary commercial targets.

### Titles
- Head of Post / Head of Production
- Managing Director / Executive Producer
- Head of VFX (facility-wide)
- COO / Operations Director (larger houses)
- Head of Innovation / Emerging Technology (in some groups)

### What They Care About
- **Cost per shot** - Does this reduce the cost of delivery?
- **Schedule risk** - Can this help us hit deadlines?
- **Capacity constraints** - Does this free up artists for higher-value work?
- **Competitive differentiation** - Will this win us bids?

### Approach
Lead with business value. Frame in terms of capacity, cost, and competitive advantage. These people think in terms of project margins and client relationships.

### Discovery Questions
- "What's your current cost breakdown for cleanup/roto work per shot?"
- "How often do late-stage client changes blow your schedule?"
- "Are capacity constraints causing you to turn down work?"
- "What would it mean to your bidding if you could deliver X% faster?"

---

## Tier 2: Technical Champions (Make It Real Internally)

These are your critical enablers. Without one of these on side, production adoption won't happen.

### Titles
- VFX Supervisor
- CG Supervisor
- Compositing Supervisor
- Head of 2D / Head of Comp
- Pipeline TD / Head of Pipeline

### What They Validate
- **Quality** - Does the output meet show standards?
- **Workflow fit** - Does this integrate with Nuke/Flame/our pipeline?
- **Failure modes** - What happens when it goes wrong?
- **Artist acceptance** - Will the team actually use this?

### Approach
Lead with capability and technical proof. Show them it works on real shots. Be honest about limitations. These people have been burned by oversold tools before.

### Discovery Questions
- "What does your current cleanup/roto pipeline look like?"
- "What tools are your compositors spending the most time in?"
- "Where do you see the biggest quality bottlenecks?"
- "Have you evaluated any AI-based tools before? What was the experience?"
- "What would it take for you to champion a new tool internally?"

---

## Tier 3: Day-to-Day Users (Drive Organic Pull)

These create bottom-up momentum. They feel the pain directly.

### Titles
- Senior Compositors
- Lead Roto / Paint Artists
- Prep Supervisors
- Sequence Leads

### What They Feel
- **Cleanup volume** - Hours spent on tedious manual work
- **Shot iteration fatigue** - Redoing work after client feedback
- **Late-stage change pressure** - Last-minute fixes under deadline

### Approach
Don't pitch to them like buyers. Let them experience the tool. If it genuinely saves them pain, they'll become your internal evangelists. They won't sign contracts - but they create internal demand.

### Discovery Questions
- "How much of your day is spent on cleanup vs. creative work?"
- "What's the most frustrating part of your current workflow?"
- "How many iterations do you typically go through per shot?"
- "What would you do with an extra 2 hours per day?"

---

## Tier 4: Procurement / Commercial (Later-Stage Only)

Engage once value is proven. **Never lead with these.**

### Titles
- Procurement Manager
- Commercial Manager
- Vendor Management

### What They Care About
- **Pricing model** - Per-shot, per-seat, per-project?
- **Contract terms** - SLA, data security, IP
- **Risk** - What happens if it doesn't work?

### Approach
Only engage after you have an Economic Buyer sponsor and a Technical Champion who's validated the tool. Procurement's job is to reduce risk and negotiate terms - you need internal advocates before you reach this stage.

## Deal Qualification Checklist

| Check | Required? | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| Economic Buyer identified | YES | Without budget authority, stuck in pilot land |
| Technical Champion engaged | YES | Without validation, adoption won't happen |
| User group interested | YES | Without pull, tool gets shelved |
| Procurement contacted | Later | Only after value proven internally |

## Anti-Patterns (What NOT To Do)

1. **Don't lead with Procurement** - They'll squeeze you on price before value is established
2. **Don't skip the Technical Champion** - EBs can sign, but TCs make it stick
3. **Don't oversell to Supervisors** - Be honest about limitations or lose trust permanently
4. **Don't pitch Users like buyers** - Let them experience, don't sell to them
5. **Don't treat all VFX houses the same** - ILM/DNEG/Framestore have very different processes than a 30-person boutique
